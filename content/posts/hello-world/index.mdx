I have this tradition of mine where, on first day of every new year, I sit down at my desk, armed with coffee and with my mind still slightly dizzy from the night before, I prepare a sort of bucket list for the next twelve months. I think about what I want to focus on over the coming time.

<PostImage src="hello-world/cat-watching-fireworks.png" alt="An orange voxel cat watching fireworks through a window" />

This year was no different. I started with noting down a point that had already appeared on the list the year before and even a year before that. “Buy a new car”. I was resurrecting my vehicle every time I needed to get its inspection renewed, paying half of its value to keep it alive. But then I drove it and it was overall not that bad (it was still driving), so I procrastinated the start of searching for a new one.

Next on the list was finishing my studies and getting master’s degree in IT Project Management. Easy. I already had my thesis almost finished and my defense was coming in just a 3 months, in late March. After that, I would finally be free from all sorts of formal education. At least for the foreseeable future.

Finally, I wrote down the third item. “Create a dev blog”. A moment later, I added: “Write and publish articles every two weeks”. I figured that if I was already in the habit of learning and studying, I could simply redirect that time and energy into writing articles and building side projects.

Boy, was I wrong. After I defended my master’s thesis, I decided to take a “short” break. It was planned to take a two or three weeks at most.

Turned out to be half a year.

The time passed on and no blog post was written. The idea never really went away, though. It kept lingering in the back of my head, because I knew there were solid reasons for doing it.

## Motivation to start a blog

From some time now, I’ve been thinking about starting a blog, where I could share my opinions and knowledge I gathered over the last few years. Why?

The main reason is to **learn and grow**. About a year ago I stumbled upon this learn in public concept and thought it would be a nice idea to create something instead of only consuming and consuming. In today’s world, where we have access to an endless stream of information, it’s very easy to spend our time absorbing things without giving our brains a chance to process and challenge them. Writing down my thoughts would allow me to organize them better and force me to dive deeper into the subjects.

Also, we learn the most when we teach others. In order for the other person to understand us, we need to understand a topic well enough to articulate the idea clearly. If I struggle to explain something, it’s probably a sign that I should learn more about it.

The need to come up with ideas for articles may also be a good trigger to create more side projects. Before I write my opinion on some framework or library, I’ll need to test it in practice. This gives me additional push to build and try new things.

The second reason to start a blog is to **share and test my opinions and ideas against a wider audience**. Maybe what I consider a good solution actually isn’t one? Maybe I overlooked a simpler approach or made a decision based on some assumption I never questioned? That’s why it’s nice to put those ideas out in the open and confront them.

Lastly, I take a lot of notes whenever I come across something interesting or learn something I don’t want to forget. Most of those notes would otherwise stay buried deep down somewhere on my Notion. I figured, I might as well share them here and maybe **the texts I created turn out to be useful for some people**. I’ll try my best to make the content here interesting, aligned with our daily developer’s jobs and accurate.

I also hope that this text might inspire you to think about creating your own corner on the internet, where you can share what you’re enthusiastic about, learn and grow along the way.

## What to expect on this blog

I plan to write here mostly about software development, especially frontend architecture, modern tooling and common problems I encounter while coding. Some posts may be more technical and focused on specific concepts or libraries, while others might just be my thoughts on general topics, such as AI or career advice.

Most of the content will probably be aimed at mid-to-senior developers, but I also want to cover the basics. It’s good to have solid fundamentals. Sure, we have AI now, but I think that makes it even more important now to be able to tell the difference between good and bad code.

I already have a few ideas for first theoretical and more practical articles and then we’ll see how it goes from there.
